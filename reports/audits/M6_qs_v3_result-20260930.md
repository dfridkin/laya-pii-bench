# D-021 result: qs_v3 on arm A (2026-09-30)

qs_v3 changes only the pii_present instructions: it names initials, postal codes, dates tied to a
person and ages over 89, and excludes coded ids. The wording was approved and committed (cb66da4)
before the run. Arm A ran on calib, test and holdout (3,603 units, no retries, no truncation).
Calib was fit on calib only and frozen at 7a5d6bb, then scored. No rewording after test (D-021).

| arm A, test | qs_v1 | qs_v2 | qs_v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUROC p(pii) | 0.778 | 0.777 | 0.509 |
| AUROC, direct / staff / quasi-only positives | 0.834 / 0.828 / 0.706 | 0.832 / 0.828 / 0.705 | 0.606 / 0.575 / 0.404 |
| calib-fit t_low at 0.995 | 0.0054 | 0.0054 | 0.2676 |
| forward rate at calib target 0.90 / 0.95 | 30.3% / 6.2% | 31.3% / 6.0% | 10.1% / 5.4% |

## Why it got worse

I checked the obvious hypothesis: naming dates makes the model flag dated, PII-free text. It is
wrong. Mean raw p(A) on the test split:

| group | n | qs_v1 | qs_v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| PII-free units with a date in the text | 28 | 0.205 | 0.506 |
| PII-free units with no date | 1834 | 0.164 | 0.576 |
| PII units | 144 | 0.307 | 0.583 |

The longer instruction lifts p(PII) for every unit to about the same level, whether or not a date
is present. Positives and negatives stop separating. The checkpoint now behaves like the
multilingual one on this question, with a general "yes" bias. Wording, and possibly prompt length,
moves zero-shot Laya a lot.

## Consequence

- qs_v1 remains the best zero-shot prompt for arm A.
- The prompt vs gold gap (quasi-only positives) is not closable by listing the categories in the
  instruction.
- For M8: fine-tuning (arm C) rather than prompt engineering is the lever for quasi-identifiers.
- Any further prompt variant needs a new DECISIONS entry, like qs_v3.

The date check used a simple regex over the test units' text: dd-Mon-yyyy, dd/mm/yyyy,
yyyy-mm-dd and "Month d, yyyy".
